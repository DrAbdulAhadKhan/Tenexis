TELOS Stress Device

A simple, precise way to X-ray test knee and ankle ligaments — before and after treatment.



The TELOS stress unit, positioned for a standardised knee examination.

What it does, in plain terms

•  It gently and precisely stresses a knee or ankle joint by a known, repeatable amount.

•  An X-ray is taken while the joint is held in this position — showing exactly how loose or stable a ligament is.

•  The digital readout displays the exact force applied, so every test is standardised and comparable.

•  The same test can be repeated after surgery to objectively show improvement.

Why hospitals use it

•  Removes guesswork from ligament laxity diagnosis — no reliance on the examiner's hand strength alone.

•  Standardises pre- and post-treatment comparison for the same patient.

•  Protects the patient — the frame positions the limb correctly, minimising repeat X-rays.

Four standard examinations



Ankle ligament stress test — lateral X-ray view



ACL stress test — 15° Lachmann position



ACL stress test — 90° position



PCL stress test — 90° with special accessory

How the examination works

1  Position

The limb is placed in the TELOS frame, which holds the joint in the correct standard position.

2  Apply force

A calibrated, gentle force is applied and shown on the digital readout — the same force every time.

3  X-ray & compare

The X-ray is taken under stress, giving an objective, repeatable measure of ligament stability.
